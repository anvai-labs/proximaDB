== History

First *visible* end-to-end ABAC enforcement: `SnapshotCatalog`/`DmlTableReader` carry the subject
from `session.user` into `scan_table_relational` (previously a parked `None`).

Decision that still applies: the startup `user` (subject assertion) is gated through the **same**
`HeaderTrustPolicy` as the startup `database` (tenant) — `check_pgwire_subject_assertion`; strict
policies reject a bare subject at handshake (SQLSTATE 28000), `anonymous`/empty ⇒ no assertion. Under
trust auth pgwire subject enforcement is *advisory* (spoofable by design, recorded as
`AuthClass::TrustAsserted`); load-bearing authentication arrived via gRPC (TD-ABAC-5) and pgwire
SCRAM (TD-PGWIRE-AUTH-1). `subject = None` deliberately does not enforce (status-quo pass-through);
making it deny-all would break system/anonymous reads and is a separate policy decision.

Follow-ons were absorbed: gRPC/REST subject threading → TD-ABAC-5; durable predicates → TD-ABAC-4;
pgwire bypasses → TD-ABAC-10; durable epochs / column masking → TD-AUTHZ-1 L2.5.

Evidence: #1324's `abac_relational_enforcement_tests`, later the live
`abac_relational_transport_e2e` pgwire ratchet (TD-ABAC-11).
