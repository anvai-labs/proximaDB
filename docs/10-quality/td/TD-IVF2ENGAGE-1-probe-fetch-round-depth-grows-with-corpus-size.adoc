= TD-IVF2ENGAGE-1: the IVF2 coarse probe's fetch-round depth grows ~linearly with corpus size

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Value
| Status | **Open**, but RETITLED and re-scoped 2026-10-08 after re-measurement. This TD was filed as "the probe engages on 1.7% of queries at N=100k, and the byte ratchet fails". **That finding was a harness artifact and is retracted** — see "What was retracted" below. What survives is a measured depth trend.
| Severity | Low-Medium — no incorrect results, no engagement collapse. The probe pays for itself at every scale measured; the open question is how its round-trip count scales.
| Component | `src/storage/engines/sst/segment_format.rs` (coarse probe arm/fallback), `tests/sift_pax_recall_ratchet_test.rs::sift_ivf2_coarse_probe_recall_ratchet`
| Evidence | Measured below on SIFT1M subsets, workstation, `--release --features io-trace`, **behind the compaction-quiescence barrier added in the same change**
| Related | TD-RDSTRAT-8 (the probe), TD-SIFTCOMPACT-1, ADR-033 (DEPTH >> BYTES ordering)

== What was retracted, and why it matters

The original filing claimed the probe engaged on **17 of 1000** queries at
N=100 000 and that the byte ratchet therefore failed. Both numbers came from runs
taken **before** the compaction-quiescence barrier existed: `compaction_ran`
reports that compaction was *enqueued*, not finished, so the measured arms raced
the compaction and the retirement of the segments they were reading.

Re-measured behind the barrier, at the original filing's own headline
configuration (N=100 000, 1000 queries, `IVF_K=64`):

[cols="1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Arm | recall@10 | range GETs/q | bytes/q | probes

| probe OFF | 0.9827 | 10 | 3 847 823 | --
| probe ON  | 0.9808 |  7 | 2 672 940 | **1000 / 1000**
|===

**1000 of 1000, not 17** — and bytes/q fall 30.5%, so the byte ratchet passes.
`ivf_whole_region_fallback` is 0 in every run at every scale. The engagement
collapse did not exist; the measurement did not have a settled layout underneath
it. The title, the severity rationale and the headline finding of the original
filing are all withdrawn, which is why the file was renamed.

The lesson is the one TD-SIFTCOMPACT-1 records independently: on this harness,
*an un-awaited background compaction can manufacture any scaling story you care
to look for*, because the first arm absorbs the race and the second does not.

== What was measured

**All figures below are post-barrier.** Each row is a full run of
`sift_ivf2_coarse_probe_recall_ratchet` with `PROXIMADB_IVF_K=64`.

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| N | queries | probes | bytes/q ON vs OFF | recall ON vs OFF | fetch_rounds per engaged query

| 20 000  | 200  | 200 / 200   | 812 434 vs 1 462 655    | 0.9880 vs 0.9905 | **1.00**
| 100 000 | 100  | 100 / 100   | 1 484 143 vs 3 724 940  | 0.9800 vs 0.9820 | **2.89**
| 100 000 | 1000 | 1000 / 1000 | 2 672 940 vs 3 847 823  | 0.9808 vs 0.9827 | **2.77**
| 333 000 | 200  | 200 / 200   | 7 209 456 vs 11 511 928 | 0.9800 vs 0.9845 | **5.95**
|===

=== The finding

**`Sigma fetch_rounds` per engaged query rises monotonically with N: 1.00 -> 2.8 -> 5.95
at 20k / 100k / 333k.** Every arm passes, the probe is a strict win on bytes at
every scale, and recall stays within tolerance — so this is not a regression. It
is a *scaling* question in the dimension ADR-033 ranks dominant: round trips, not
bytes. A probe that halves bytes while tripling dependent fetch rounds is not
obviously a win on object storage, where DEPTH is the dominant cost term.

Two quantities that look like findings and are not:

* `Sigma cells_total / engaged` is **64** and `cells_probed / engaged` is **16** in
  every run — i.e. "25% of cells probed" is `nprobe / k_c` = 16/64, a *configured
  constant*, not a measurement. (`geometric_nprobe(64) = ceil(sqrt(64) * 2.0) = 16`.)
* The probe arm's bytes/q is **not** invariant to query count even post-barrier:
  1 484 143 at q=100 vs 2 672 940 at q=1000, same N. The barrier removed the
  layout race, not this. Most likely cache warm-up/eviction over the longer run,
  which is also why the arms must be run in BOTH orders before any figure here is
  ledgered — the baseline arm runs first and cold, the probe arm second and warm.

== Two guards that were too weak either way

Both were genuinely too weak, independent of the retracted finding: a presence
check certifies a probe that is effectively off, whatever the true engagement
turns out to be. They are strengthened in the same change.

1. **`assert!(!probe_trace.is_empty(), "... must engage the coarse probe ...")`**
   would be satisfied by 17 probes out of 1000 queries. A presence check certifies a probe that is
   effectively off. Replaced in this change by a **proportion** floor (>=50% of
   measured queries), which is what the assertion's own message always claimed
   to check.
2. **The probe's error path is swallowed.** `segment_format.rs` calls
   `coarse_probe_survivors(...).await.ok().flatten()`, so `Err` and
   `Ok(None)` both become "no probe" and take the same fallback. From the
   recorded metrics — `record_ivf_probe_durable(0, 0, 0, 0, true)` for every miss
   — a probe that *failed* is indistinguishable from one that legitimately
   *declined*. Until that is split, a miss cannot be attributed to a reason.
   (An earlier revision said "the 983 misses" -- 1000 minus 17, carried over from
   the retracted measurement. Engagement is 100% at every scale measured and
   `ivf_whole_region_fallback` is 0, so there are currently no misses to
   attribute at all; see "How to apply" item 3.)

== How to apply

. **Characterise the fetch-round growth, which is the only live finding.**
  `fetch_rounds` per engaged query measured 1.00 / 2.8 / 5.95 at N =
  20k / 100k / 333k. Establish whether that is linear in N, in rows-per-cell, or
  in `k_c`: `IVF_K=64` gives ~310 rows/cell at 20k against ~1 560 at 100k and
  ~5 200 at 333k, so a probe that must touch most cells to reach top-k is the
  obvious hypothesis and `nprobe`/`k_c` sizing is the obvious lever. Note that
  `cells_probed / cells_total` is a CONFIGURED 16/64 and cannot speak to this.

. **Re-measure at 1M, which is now possible.** The harness previously failed
  deterministically at N >= ~333k: the first flush crossed the 32 MB training
  floor, so a training compaction consumed the only L0 and the second flush had
  nothing due (`compaction_ran` false, `compaction_error: None` -- a missing
  enqueue with no error to point at). The test now pins
  `PROXIMADB_TRAINING_COMPACTION_MIN_MB` so only the count arm fires. Run the
  arms in BOTH orders, because the baseline arm currently runs first and cold
  while the probe arm runs second and warm.

. **Then, and only if the growth is a real cost, split the miss reason.**
  `segment_format.rs` calls `coarse_probe_survivors(...).await.ok().flatten()`,
  collapsing ~15 descriptive `Err` paths and **six** `return Ok(None)` declines
  (`:3090`, `:3107`, `:3112`, `:3124`, `:3173`, `:3194` on `origin/develop`;
  `coarse_probe_survivors` starts at `:3067`. Those are `origin/develop` numbers
  and the PR shifts them by **+2** -- it edits a comment higher up in that file,
  taking it from 9601 to 9603 lines -- so in the PR tree read
  :3092/:3109/:3114/:3126/:3175/:3196 with the function at :3069. An earlier revision of this parenthetical said the PR does not touch the
  file, which stopped being true when it did) into one "no probe", with
  `record_ivf_probe_durable(0,0,0,0,true)` firing for both. This was item 1 while
  the engagement collapse was believed real; with engagement measured at 100% at
  every scale there are currently **no misses to attribute**, so it is
  speculative work until a measurement says otherwise.

. **Do not relax the byte ratchet.** `bytes_probe < bytes_base` is the correct
  contract -- a probe that reads more bytes than the scan it replaces has no
  reason to exist -- and it now PASSES at every scale measured (-44% at 20k,
  -30% at 100k/1000q, -37% at 333k). An earlier revision of this TD said the
  test "being red at the default N is the honest state"; it is green, and the red
  was the un-awaited compaction.
