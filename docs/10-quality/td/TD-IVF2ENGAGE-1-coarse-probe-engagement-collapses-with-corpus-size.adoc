= TD-IVF2ENGAGE-1: the IVF2 coarse probe engages on 1.7% of queries at N=100k, and the byte ratchet fails

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Value
| Status | **Open.** Filed 2026-10-08, the first time this eval could be run.
| Severity | Medium — no incorrect results; the probe silently stops paying for itself at scale, and two guards were too weak to notice.
| Component | `src/storage/engines/sst/segment_format.rs` (coarse probe arm/fallback), `tests/sift_pax_recall_ratchet_test.rs::sift_ivf2_coarse_probe_recall_ratchet`
| Evidence | Measured below on SIFT1M subsets, workstation, `--release --features io-trace`
| Related | TD-RDSTRAT-8 (the probe), ADR-033 (DEPTH ≫ BYTES ordering)

== What was measured

`sift_ivf2_coarse_probe_recall_ratchet` could not run at all until the catalog
object-id fix in this change — it failed the flush/compaction-admission
precondition, and because it is `#[ignore]`d CI never surfaced that. Run for the
first time, it reports a result that **inverts with corpus size**.

N=20 000, 200 queries, `IVF_K=64`:

[cols="1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Arm | recall@10 | range GETs/q | bytes/q

| probe OFF | 0.9884 | 3 | 6 316 695
| probe ON  | 0.9905 | 2 | 5 861 939
|===

`probes_recorded=200/200`, `Σcells_total=12800 Σcells_probed=3200
Σfetch_rounds=200`. Better recall, **−33% round trips**, −7.2% bytes — a strict
win, no trade.

=== Measurement hygiene: the baseline arm is nondeterministic; the cause is NOT established

The two regimes are real and reproducible. The *cause* is not, and an earlier
revision of this TD asserted one it could not support.

What replicates:

* **The probe arm is bit-identical.** Across every run observed — six by me and
  four independently, spanning loadavg 2.5 to 11.0 — the probe arm returns the
  same recall, the same GET count and the same byte count to the byte. It is not
  sensitive to machine state at all.
* **The baseline arm is not.** Two shapes appear: ~3 GETs with ~6.3 MB, and
  ~5–7 GETs with ~1.5–1.6 MB. More round trips for fewer bytes — a different
  read *plan*, not noise in the same plan.
* **Baseline recall moves on its own.** Independently measured at 0.9730 /
  0.9780 / 0.9780 / 0.9790 across four runs with no deliberate load
  manipulation — a wider spread than the 0.9905 -> 0.9880 an earlier revision
  attributed to saturation.

What does NOT replicate — and what this TD previously claimed:

* "The figures move with CPU load." Retracted. The outlier regime was observed
  at loadavg **7.4** and *absent* at loadavg **11.0**; the specific sentence "a
  moderate load (loadavg ~4) does not reproduce it; it takes real saturation" is
  refuted. One sample per regime cannot distinguish load-dependence from
  plain run-to-run nondeterminism, and the data is consistent with the latter.
* An earlier revision before that blamed **L0 segment count** and a wall-clock
  second boundary in segment naming. Also retracted, and self-inflicted: segment
  names are `L{level}_{%Y%m%dT%H%M%S}_{uuid8}`
  (`compaction_orchestrator.rs:662-667`), so the uuid makes a same-second
  collision impossible; the "one vs two L0" reading came from a grep whose
  pattern stopped before the uuid. Segment count was constant in both regimes.

So this TD has now been wrong about the cause twice, in both cases by inferring
a mechanism from a single pair of observations. The honest statement is: **the
baseline arm's read plan and recall are nondeterministic, and nothing here
identifies why.**

Candidate mechanisms, none tested, all with a named entry point:

. **Adaptive search degree.** `InFlightSearchGuard::acquire()` feeds
  `adaptive_degree(base, IN_FLIGHT_SEARCHES)` (`search/mod.rs:142-148`, `:2191`)
  — concurrency-sensitive by construction, which would make the plan depend on
  what else is in flight.
. **Bounded-concurrent `read_ranges`** (TD-RDSTRAT-12), whose `fetch_rounds` /
  `max_inflight` decide how ranges coalesce. A plan issuing 7 smaller reads
  instead of 3 large ones is exactly the observed signature.
. **The measurement itself may be lying.** `IO_TRACE` is a `task_local!`, and its
  own doc comment warns that "a `task_local!` does **not** cross
  `tokio::spawn`, so those tasks must record through a captured handle"
  (TD-OLAP-3,
  `crates/modalities/proximadb-observability-engine/src/io_trace.rs:76-82`).
  `measure()` wraps the whole query loop in ONE `io_trace::scope`, so a spawned
  scan worker drops its bytes from the total — which presents as "bytes down,
  GETs accounted elsewhere". **Rule this out first:** if the instrument is
  nondeterministic, every number above is suspect, including the ones that look
  stable.

Consequence for anyone using this harness: state the machine state alongside any
figure, and treat a baseline-arm delta smaller than ~0.5% recall or ~5x bytes as
indistinguishable from run-to-run variation until the above is resolved. None of
these numbers is in `BENCHMARK_EVIDENCE.toml`, and none should be until then.

== The finding

**What actually changes is how often the probe runs.** 200 of 200 queries record
a probe at N=20k; 17 of 1000 at N=100k. The rest take the armed-but-missed
**whole-Region-A fallback**, which is the GET budget the probe exists to avoid,
and engagement that thin cannot pay for its own footer work — which is why bytes
go *up* at 100k.

=== Correction: the "constant prune ratio" is not a measurement

An earlier revision of this TD made the 25%-probed-at-both-scales figure its
headline — "corpus size does not degrade the probe's selectivity" — and derived
from it that "any fix aimed at `nprobe`, `IVF_K` or the read budget is aimed at
the wrong quantity". **Both are wrong, and the ratio is forced by configuration
rather than measured.**

Per engaged query, `cells_total` is `k_c`, and the test pins `PROXIMADB_IVF_K=64`
(12 800/200 = 64; 1 088/17 = 64). `cells_probed` is `coarse_probe_nprobe(64)`,
and the test sets no `PROXIMADB_PAX_READ_COARSE_NPROBE`, so it falls to
`geometric_nprobe(64, defaults)` = `ceil(sqrt(64) × 2.0)` = **16**
(`default_nprobe_multiplier() = 2.0`, `nprobe_max = 0` = uncapped) — confirmed
3 200/200 = 16 and 272/17 = 16.

16/64 = 25% at *every* corpus size, by construction. Two data points of a
constant carry no information about selectivity, and the derived directive is
backwards: `nprobe` and `IVF_K` are precisely the two quantities that set the
ratio. The earlier revision even contradicted itself, listing `IVF_K=64` giving
~1 560 rows/cell as a leading candidate cause one section later.

=== The signal that WAS in the data and went unanalysed

`Σfetch_rounds` per engaged query **doubled**: 200/200 = 1 at N=20k, 34/17 = 2 at
N=100k. That is a depth regression in the dimension ADR-033 ranks dominant, and
it contradicts "only engagement changes" — among the queries that *do* engage,
each now costs twice the round trips. It is the one number here that is measured
rather than configured, and it should be the first thing the work below explains.

`probed_rows` — the third field of `record_probe_trace`'s 4-tuple, and the only
one that could carry real selectivity information — is not reported by the
harness at all. Print it before drawing any further conclusion about pruning.

So the knee is not in `nprobe` and not in the budget. It is in whether the probe
engages at all, and that is the quantity to instrument before tuning anything —
"trace before you tune" (the co-design mandate) applied to the probe itself.

NOTE: this also explains an earlier vacuous sweep. Varying
`PROXIMADB_PAX_READ_COARSE_NPROBE` across 32/16/8/4 and fully-off produced
byte-identical results (53.97 GETs, 25 297 445 bytes in every arm) because the
segments were not clustered, so nothing was probing. A knob sweep over an
unengaged probe measures nothing; neither does one over an untrained index.

== Two guards that were too weak to catch it

1. **`assert!(!probe_trace.is_empty(), "... must engage the coarse probe ...")`**
   is satisfied by 17/1000. A presence check certifies a probe that is
   effectively off. Replaced in this change by a **proportion** floor (>=50% of
   measured queries), which is what the assertion's own message always claimed
   to check.
2. **The probe's error path is swallowed.** `segment_format.rs` calls
   `coarse_probe_survivors(...).await.ok().flatten()`, so `Err` and
   `Ok(None)` both become "no probe" and take the same fallback. From the
   recorded metrics — `record_ivf_probe_durable(0, 0, 0, 0, true)` for every miss
   — a probe that *failed* is indistinguishable from one that legitimately
   *declined*. Until that is split, the 983 misses cannot be attributed.

== How to apply

. **Split the miss reason first**, before changing any policy. `segment_format.rs`
  calls `coarse_probe_survivors(...).await.ok().flatten()`, collapsing ~15
  descriptive `Err` paths and **six** `return Ok(None)` declines (`:3090`,
  `:3107`, `:3112`, `:3124`, `:3173`, `:3194`) into one "no probe",
  and `record_ivf_probe_durable(0,0,0,0,true)` fires for both. Nothing else
  distinguishes them: the only `warn!` on that path is an unrelated non-fatal
  footer-overlap case, and there is no error counter. Record the error distinctly
  from the decline — AND record WHICH decline, since the six reasons are also
  mutually indistinguishable, so splitting `Err` alone will not attribute the
  983. Until then every hypothesis below is unfalsifiable.
. **Report `probed_rows` and per-query `fetch_rounds`.** The harness prints
  neither per query. `fetch_rounds` already shows the doubling above and is the
  one measured signal here; `probed_rows` is the only field that can speak to
  selectivity at all.
. Then attribute the 983, with candidates ordered by cost to test: multiple
  segments after compaction where only some carry a trained A0 directory (which
  would also explain the doubled `fetch_rounds`, and makes the per-segment-read
  nature of `probe_trace` load-bearing); a footer-plan cache miss making the
  probe decline rather than pay an extra GET; and `k_c`/`nprobe` sizing —
  `IVF_K=64` gives ~1 560 rows/cell at 100k against ~310 at 20k, so a probe that
  must touch most cells to reach top-k. That last one is a genuine candidate, NOT
  excluded by the 25% figure — see the correction above.
. **Do not relax the byte ratchet to make the test green.** `bytes_probe <
  bytes_base` is the correct contract: a probe that reads more bytes than the
  scan it replaces has no reason to exist. The test being red at the default N is
  the honest state, and it is `#[ignore]`d so no CI tier is affected.
. Re-measure at 20k/100k/333k/1M once engagement holds, and only then sweep
  `nprobe`. Record in `BENCHMARK_EVIDENCE.toml` per mandate #6 — the numbers above
  are workstation-only and deliberately not ledgered as `measured`.
