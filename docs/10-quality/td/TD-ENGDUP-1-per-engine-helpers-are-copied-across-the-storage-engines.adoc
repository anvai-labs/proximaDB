= TD-ENGDUP-1: 16 helper bodies are copied across the storage engines, and a generic copy already exists

[cols="1,3"]
|===
| Status | **Open.** Filed 2026-10-09 from a structural sweep of the v0.4.0 tree
(`develop` @ `9d6acc7f4`).
| Severity | Low-to-medium. Behaviour is correct today and **no drift has been demonstrated** --
an earlier revision of this file claimed two pieces of drift evidence and both were measurement
artifacts (see "Measurement notes"). The cost is maintenance surface: a fix to one engine's copy
does not reach the others, and 40 copies sit where one shared definition would do.
| Owner | unassigned

Only **three** sets live wholly inside `progressive_stages.rs`; `filter_candidates` is there in
six of its seven sites; the remaining twelve are spread across the engine trees and the
DataFusion adapters. That distribution is what determines the shape of the fix, and an earlier
revision of this TD got it wrong by assuming all sixteen were in one file.

=== The per-engine `progressive_stages.rs` files

`src/storage/engines/<engine>/progressive_stages.rs` exists once per engine for **1,721 LOC**:

|===
| Engine | LOC | Identical non-blank lines shared with the SST copy

| sst    | 271 | (baseline, 235 non-blank)
| nova   | 267 | 192 / 231 (83%)
| swift  | 273 | 194 / 236 (82%)
| viper  | 268 | 192 / 232 (83%)
| helix  | 302 | 194 / 263 (74%)
| raptor | 340 | 174 / 298 (58%)
|===

Those percentages count any identical non-blank line anywhere in the file, which includes
punctuation-only lines -- for nova, 42 of the 192 matches are braces and 7 are comments, leaving
143 substantive. On a substantive-only basis the range is 59-85% rather than 58-83%. The headline
is not overstated, but the measure is looser than "identical lines" suggests.

`swift/stages.rs` (822 LOC, compiled via `swift/mod.rs:190`) shares 179 of its lines with
`swift/progressive_stages.rs` and is **excluded** from the 1,721 total, so the duplication in
that engine is larger than the table shows.

== The finding that should drive the fix

**A generic, already-compiled copy of these stages exists in-tree.**
`src/storage/engines/core/progressive/stage.rs` (535 LOC, compiled via
`src/storage/engines/core/mod.rs:19 pub mod progressive;`) defines `BinaryStage`, `Int8Stage`,
`PqStage` and friends implementing `ProgressiveSearchStage` -- and its `compute_distances` bodies
are **byte-identical** to the per-engine ones (Binary variant at `stage.rs:229`, Int8 at
`stage.rs:302`), as are `new`, `quantization_level` and `can_skip`.

So the generic abstraction is not hypothetical future work: it is written, compiled, and already
duplicated by the six engine copies. Each cross-engine count above is therefore understated by
one. The question is not "should we build a shared stage driver" but "why do the engines not use
the one that exists", and that is what the first slice should answer.

== Why consolidating is worth doing

. **40 copies is 40 places to keep a fix.** No drift has been observed, but the surface is real,
  and `create_test_engines` being duplicated six ways means the test fixtures can drift
  independently -- the hazard mandate #11 exists to prevent.
. **It is a cheap reduction of `src/storage`.** That module is 295,958 LOC -- 37% of the root
  crate -- and 26 of the 41 top-level modules under `src/` reference `crate::storage::`. Shared
  helpers are the part with no engine-specific semantics, so they carry the least behavioural
  risk per line removed.
. **The DataFusion adapter copies matter more than their size.** `new` and
  `supports_filters_pushdown` are duplicated across the three `*_adapter.rs` files, and ADR-039
  constrains what may live behind that seam -- three copies means three places to keep the lane
  discipline right.

== Options

. **Answer the `engines/core/progressive/stage.rs` question first.** Determine why each engine
  carries its own stage file when a generic one is compiled: genuine per-engine divergence
  (RAPTOR's Int8 stage, ~78 lines, does look engine-specific), or drift from a migration that was
  never finished. This is a reading task, not a refactor, and it decides whether option 2 is a
  move or a redesign. Nothing else should start before it.
. **Lift the twelve non-`progressive_stages` helpers** to a shared home. The bodies are
  byte-identical, so the first pass is a move plus a `use`.
. **Then collapse the stage files** according to what option 1 found.
. **Guard it.** The duplicate-body detector used here is a content hash over normalised function
  bodies; baselining the cross-engine clone count and ratcheting it down stops the class
  regrowing.

On the destination: `proximadb-engine-core` is the natural candidate because the root crate
already depends on it (`Cargo.toml:775`) and it describes itself as "Shared engine-core ops +
formats hoisted out of the root crate" -- so no new dependency edge is introduced. Note its
fan-out of 20 (rank 2 of 152) is the number of crates it *depends on*, not the number depending
on it; its fan-in is 2, so it is not yet a widely shared destination and lifting into it pulls 20
edges into scope.

== Verification

* Byte-identity of each lifted body is the precondition: the move must be a pure relocation,
  diffable before and after on the normalised body.
* `cargo nx` and `cargo build --profile release-server` green. The ANN recall ratchets
  (`sift_pax_cascade_recall_at_10_ratchet`, `tests/sift_pax_recall_ratchet_test.rs:575`, and
  `sift_pax_filtered_cascade_recall_ratchet`, `:2472`) unchanged -- `compute_distances` and
  `filter_candidates` are on the search path, so a behavioural change there moves recall.
* `scripts/check-layering.sh` and `scripts/check_workspace_boundaries.py` green.
* `make work-commit-check` green.

== Measurement notes

An earlier revision of this TD asserted two pieces of drift evidence. **Both were artifacts, and
both are retracted:**

* **"`convert_filter_to_index` ... raptor differs by two characters"** -- measured with a fixed
  60-line window from the `fn` line, not the function's extent. At the real brace-matched extent
  helix, nova, raptor and viper are all **identical** (md5 `af8321cc98`, 59 lines, 1,581
  normalised chars); the two characters were the start of the *next* function. The function is
  also defined in **six** engines, not five -- `sst/search/mod.rs:1171` was omitted, and its body
  differs (1,674 chars), as does SWIFT's (`swift/engine.rs:834`, 1,365 chars, 56 lines). SWIFT and
  SST genuinely differ; raptor does not. The table above was right while that prose was wrong,
  which should have been caught by reading one against the other.
* **"`compute_distances` exists in two different identical-body variants ... which is what
  partial propagation looks like"** -- the two "variants" are the `BinaryStage` and `Int8Stage`
  trait impls, two distinct methods on two distinct structs that coexist **in the same file in
  every engine** (`sst/progressive_stages.rs:57` and `:128`, with a third at `:186` for Fp32).
  They differ because one hamming-scores binary codes and the other dequantises int8. The 6-vs-5
  gap is only that RAPTOR's Int8 stage is engine-specific. No partial propagation is demonstrated.

Also worth recording: matching function **name+signature** rather than body reports ~26,000
"duplicated" lines in this repo, because `fn main()` and `validate` recur with entirely
different bodies. Hashing normalised **bodies** restricted to non-test files with bodies of 16+
lines gives 52-53 cross-file clone sets and roughly 1,800-1,900 removable LOC repo-wide; that
threshold is load-bearing and six of the sixteen rows above fall below it, so the sets here are
the largest coherent *cross-engine* cluster rather than the largest sets overall.
