== The defect

`ivf_projection_dims(dim, k) = max(1.5·log2 dim, 1.5·log2 k)` decides how many PCA
components rank coarse cells at query time. It yields **10 components for 128-d**
and **14 for 768-d** — 7.8% and 1.8% of the space respectively. When the true
nearest cell fails to rank inside `nprobe` under that compressed metric, no amount
of centroid precision recovers it: the cell is simply never read.

Measured cost of that under-projection, at the recall-0.98 SLA:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| corpus | default | optimum | floor nprobe | GET/query | write
| 128-d BIGANN 3.3M (k_c=100) | 10 | **32** | 16 → 12 | 33.4 → **26.3** (−21%) | +15%
| 768-d BGE 491k (k_c=90) | 14 | **64** | 30 → 25 | 48.6 → **38.7** (−20%) | +8.9%
|===

Both optima beat *every* other width measured at their scale, on every shared
point. The 768-d saving applies at top-10 and top-20 alike; the 128-d saving is
−21%/−23%.

[WARNING]
====
**The specific optima in this table are not reproducible and must not be used to
set a default.** Every row was measured on a single clustering draw. Replicating
the 768-d row at a second seed reverses the 32-vs-64 ordering and erases the
top-20 advantage entirely — while a same-seed rebuild reproduces to the digit, so
the variance is the *draw*, not the measurement. A width-14 control at the second
draw puts the best available saving at **6.1%/5.0%**, achieved by width **32**,
against the 20.3%/19.6% by width **64** in this table. Both the magnitude and the
optimal width are draw-dependent. Full data and what survives:
<<seed-replication>>.
====

[IMPORTANT]
====
**Widening the projection does not, on its own, bank those GET savings.** Every
figure above is measured **at the recall-0.98 floor**, i.e. with `nprobe` re-tuned
per width. Production does not re-tune: `coarse_probe_nprobe` uses
`geometric_nprobe = ceil(sqrt(k_c) × multiplier)`, which has **no `n_comp` term**
— it is width-blind.

So at the shipped `nprobe`, a wider projection buys **recall per probe**, not fewer
requests. That is still the right first move, for two reasons:

* It is a **recall fix today.** The sqrt policy is BIGANN-fitted and
  under-provisions neural corpora at identical `k_c` — both BGE beds sit **below
  the 0.98 SLA** (0.967 and 0.976), as the nprobe-policy section below records.
* It is the **prerequisite** for converting the frontier gain into COGS: a wider
  projection is what makes a *lower* `nprobe` viable at the same recall.

The `nprobe` retune is therefore a deliberate follow-up, kept separate so this
change stays one-variable against the probe-economy ratchet. Quoting the −20%
without the retune would overstate what shipping the floor alone delivers.
====

=== Two structural faults, separable from the calibration

**(a) The `k` term is masked and far too shallow.** `max(…)` means the `k` term
only exceeds the `dim` term when `k_c > dim`. Every scale we measured sits below
that, so `n_comp` is pinned by dimension alone and **never responds to `k_c`** —
yet `k_c` is the variable that actually drives the requirement. Forcing `k_c`
apart from `N` shows it accounts for ~¾ of the effect (raising `k_c` at fixed N
moves the 10-d penalty by 0.0092; tripling N at fixed `k_c` moves it by 0.0032).
The transition sits between `k_c` 30 and 50 — about **1.5M vectors** — so nearly
every production collection needs a wider projection, and SIFT1M-scale benchmarks
(k_c=30, where all widths tie) show nothing at all.

NOTE: an earlier revision of this TD argued the `k` term had the *wrong sign*.
Measurement refuted that — the requirement rises with `k_c`, because more cells
sit closer together and ranking them needs finer resolution. The direction is
right; the magnitude and the `max()` masking are the faults.

**(b) `dim` is the wrong primary variable.** Intrinsic content is nearly flat
across a controlled BGE ladder — 384→1024 is 2.7× wider but needs only 1.20× the
components for 79% variance (132→158). A rule keyed on `log2(dim)` scales with
the one quantity that demonstrably does not drive the answer.

== What we could NOT predict

Three a-priori framings were each fitted to the 128-d data and each failed
out-of-sample at 768-d:

[cols="2,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| framing | predicted 768-d optimum | actual
| samples/param ≥ ~15 | 32 | **wrong** — 64 beat 32 by +0.0190 (11/11 points)
| fraction of dims ≈ 25% | ~190 | **wrong** — flat from 64 to 128 (−0.0014, 6/13)
| variance target 79% | 163 | **wrong** — flat well below it
|===

The samples/param framing failed most instructively: at 128-d the optimum sat at
s/p 15.6 and 64 components *lost*; at 768-d, 64 components *won* at s/p 8.5. So
`S_min` was an artifact of where 128-d happens to sit on its own variance curve,
not a portable constant. The two-constraint clamp rule this TD previously proposed
(a variance target AND an independent estimation floor) is therefore **wrong in
structure**, not merely mis-parameterised: the two terms trade off continuously.

The interpolation this TD previously offered — `n_comp ~= 4.9 * dim^0.387`,
predicting ~49 at 384-d — has since been **measured and refuted**. See the
factorial below.

== The factorial this TD asked for (completed 2026-08-12)

384-d BGE, same 491,655 chunks as the 768-d bed, `k_c` FORCED to 90 so embedding
dimension is the only variable:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| n_comp | % of dim | s/p | floor@10 | GET/q | floor@20 | GET/q
| 12 (default) | 3.1% | 45.5 | 30 | 34.6 | 30 | 34.4
| **24** | 6.2% | 22.8 | **25** | **29.6** | 30 | 32.0
| 32 | 8.3% | 17.1 | 25 | 29.4 | 30 | 32.5
| 48 | 12.5% | 11.4 | 25 | 29.4 | 30 | 33.0
| **64** | 16.7% | 8.5 | 25 | 29.4 | **25** | **28.6**
|===

Sub-0.98 mean deltas: `24 vs 12 = +0.0198`, `32 vs 24 = +0.0019`,
`48 vs 32 = +0.0023`, `64 vs 48 = +0.0219`.

**The curve is two-stepped, not smooth.** A large gain 12→24, a **plateau across
24/32/48**, then a second gain at 64. So the interpolation's prediction of 49 lands
squarely inside the dead zone — 48 is measurably no better than 24, at twice the
width. Dimension is not the policy key.

NOTE: quote the **sub-0.98** statistic, not the full-grid mean. Above the floor
every width converges to 0.994–0.998 (the TD-WLP-4b saturation), and averaging
those ties in dilutes real effects by 30–40%.

=== `k_c` does not move the optimum

768-d, the same corpus, `k_c` forced apart from N:

[cols="1,1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| k_c | MiB/cell | width | floor@10 | GET/q | 64-vs-32 (sub-0.98)
| 30 | 12.0 | 32 | 12 | 49.5 | —
| 30 | 12.0 | 64 | 10 | 39.7 | **+0.0221**
| 90 | 4.0 | 32 | 25 | 41.0 | —
| 90 | 4.0 | 64 | 25 | 38.7 | **+0.0151**
|===

64 wins at both cell counts, so **the width policy needs no `k_c` term**. A second
effect appears though: a wider projection largely *cancels* the penalty for coarse
cells. At `n_comp=32`, moving 90→30 costs +21% GET/query; at `n_comp=64` it costs
**+2.6%**. Width buys robustness to `k_c` misconfiguration, not just probes.

=== The best invariant found: between-centroid variance

Coarse ranking needs the ability to separate *centroids*, which is not what PCA
maximises. Measuring `eta^2 = between-centroid variance / total variance` per
component (60k sample, k-means in the full PCA space):

[cols="1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| corpus | measured optimum | % BETWEEN-centroid | % total variance
| 128-d BIGANN | 32 | 96.8% | 79.1%
| 384-d BGE | 64 | 92.4% | 59.5%
| 768-d BGE | 64 | 93.1% | 56.9%
| *spread* | | *4.4 pp* | *22.2 pp*
|===

**5× tighter than total variance**, and within one model family essentially exact
(92.4 vs 93.1 — 0.7 pp, at different ambient dims). It is still **not** a single
threshold: `T_b=92%` predicts BIGANN at 24 (measured 32); `T_b=96%` predicts both
BGE at 128 (measured 64). BIGANN sits ~4 pp higher — a corpus-family effect.

Consequence for any future rule: **target between-centroid variance, not total
variance.** `T=0.79` of total variance demands ~147 components at 384-d, while 64
already captures 92.4% of what ranking actually needs. Targeting the wrong
quantity is why earlier drafts of this TD over-demanded width and wrongly
concluded that neural embeddings were infeasible under the training cap. The
quantity is computable at compaction time from the centroids and projection the
engine already holds.

REFUTED, recorded so it is not retried: the 48→64 step is *not* explained by
discriminative directions arriving late in the PCA ordering. Between-variance
added by components 49–64 versus 33–48 is a ratio of 0.55, against a
total-variance ratio of 0.77 — i.e. those components contribute *less* separation
per unit variance, the opposite of what that hypothesis requires. The step remains
unexplained; the spectrum, the eta^2 profile and cell balance are all smooth, and
beds are deterministic.

=== The sqrt-based nprobe policy is BIGANN-specific

`ceil(1.7*sqrt(k_c))` = 17 at `k_c`≈90–100:

[cols="2,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| bed | k_c | policy | measured floor | verdict
| 128-d BIGANN | 100 | 17 | 12 | MEETS (over-provisions)
| 384-d BGE | 90 | 17 | 25 | **MISSES** (0.9672 @ np20)
| 768-d BGE | 90 | 17 | 25 | **MISSES** (0.9758 @ np20)
|===

Probe requirement is not a function of cell count alone — embedding geometry
matters. A policy fitted on BIGANN under-provisions neural corpora at identical
`k_c`, landing below the 0.98 SLA.

=== Why a retune is not a one-line change

Three structural facts, recorded so the retune is scoped from evidence rather
than from the formula's shape:

* **`nprobe` is process-global.** `coarse_probe_nprobe(k_c)` takes only the cell
  count — no collection, no tenant, no query
  (`src/storage/engines/sst/segment_format.rs`). A per-collection or
  per-corpus-class policy is a signature change through
  `coarse_probe_survivors`, not a config edit. `IndexPolicyInput.nprobe`
  (ADR-028) and `SearchEffort` look like they would serve, but both feed the
  **AXIS warm-index** path and never reach the segment probe. TD-RDSTRAT-9 is
  the open TD for the missing collection read-cost controller.
* **The signal that would drive a controller is already emitted and read by
  nothing.** `proximadb_ivf_whole_region_fallback_total` is documented as
  "climbing ⇒ nprobe too small" (`src/storage/engines/sst/metrics.rs`), but no
  code consumes it. It is observability without a control loop.
* **The multiplier's own justification is weaker than it reads.** See the
  corrected note on `nprobe_multiplier` in `src/core/config.rs`: the 6-cell and
  11-cell arms were measured under *different read planners* (local vs Azure),
  so the request-cost half of that comparison is confounded, and the recall
  figure previously quoted in the comment (0.9786) does not appear in the source
  sweep at all (which reads 0.9795).

== Where the GET cost actually is (measured 2026-08-16)

The exact-current paired rerun TD-SEARCH-3 required is complete
(`runs/range-cap-current-eb997aa6e`, binary `926900df04a5…` @ `eb997aa6e`,
`status: pass`, promotion-eligible on all 8 checks: wire GETs −63.8%/−64.0%,
bytes ×1.033/1.040, p50 ×0.398/0.402, p95 ×1.001/1.048, RSS ×1.064).

Decomposing it moves the target of this TD.

[cols="1,1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| cap | GET/q | Region-A rounds | non-A GETs | Region-B MB/q
| 4 MiB | 49.54 | 8.80 | 40.75 | 143.3
| 24 MiB | 17.96 | 7.43 (−15.6%) | 10.53 (**−74.2%**) | 145.7
|===

**Region A — the probe-cell path this TD's projection width governs, and the one
ADR-065's "one cell ≈ one IOP" premise is about — is 18% of GETs and improves
15.6%.** The entire saving is in the **rerank tier (Region B) and the OID blocks
(Region D)**, which no width or `k_c` change touches.

Corroborated structurally: D blocks are sized `DEFAULT_TARGET_BLOCK_SIZE_BYTES`
= 3 MiB against a 4 MiB Azure cap, so `3 + 3 > 4` and **block coalescing merges
nothing on Azure today**; at 24 MiB it merges 8. The same
`MAX_TARGET_BLOCK_SIZE_BYTES` = 4 MiB clamp makes S3's 8 MiB target unreachable
as a block size.

Consequences for this TD, stated plainly:

* The projection-width programme optimised the **cheap** tier's geometry. Region
  B is 143 MB/q of 162 (88% of bytes) and ~82% of requests.
* Width remains worth shipping — it is a **recall** fix at the shipped `nprobe`,
  where both neural beds sit below the 0.98 SLA — but it should no longer be
  described as the lever on GET/query. It is not.
* The `k_c` U-curve recorded above is a curve in Region-A geometry under a fixed
  4 MiB planner. Both conditions now look load-bearing.

The cap decision itself belongs to **TD-SEARCH-3**, which owns the promotion gate
and still requires the `k_c=90` repeat at an eligible cap. Nothing here
authorizes a default change.

== Seed replication — the 768-d width ordering does NOT reproduce (measured 2026-08-22)

Flip precondition 1 has been run and it **fails**. At 768-d the 32-vs-64 ordering
is a property of the clustering draw, not of the geometry.

Both beds were rebuilt at `PROXIMADB_IVF_KMEANS_SEED=987654321` with everything
else held: same corpus, `k_c`=90, same binary (`56a780e43db8…` @ `ea93b2e9c`), same
1000-query slice against exact top-100 ground truth, same nprobe grid. The
persisted `coarse_seed` was **asserted on each bed before sweeping** — this repo
has already shipped one env gate (`PROXIMADB_IVF_NCOMP`) that was accepted and
silently ignored, and an unverified gate here would have produced two identical
beds and a confident, meaningless "no sensitivity" result.

.Floor = lowest nprobe on the grid reaching recall ≥ 0.98; cells are `recall / GET-per-query`
[cols="1,2,2,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| | w32, default seed | w64, default seed | w32, seed 987654321 | w64, seed 987654321
| top-10 floor | np25 — 0.9821 / 41.03 | np25 — 0.9858 / **38.68** | np25 — 0.9837 / **40.21** | np25 — 0.9820 / 42.08
| top-20 floor | np30 — 0.9864 / 48.01 | **np25** — 0.9835 / 39.00 | np30 — 0.9862 / 47.18 | np30 — 0.9855 / 49.96
|===

Two independent reversals:

* **The top-20 floor advantage disappears.** At the default draw width 64 clears
  0.98 a full grid step earlier than width 32 (np25 vs np30) — the single
  strongest number behind the width recommendation, and the whole of the −19.6%
  top-20 saving. At the alternate draw both widths floor at np30.
* **The top-10 request ordering inverts.** Width 64 is 5.7% *cheaper* than width
  32 at the default draw and 4.6% *dearer* at the alternate one. This is not a
  near-tie that tipped: at the default seed width 64 has lower GET/query at
  **every** nprobe measured, and at the alternate seed it has higher GET/query at
  **every** nprobe measured. The entire curve moves, not one point.

=== This is not measurement noise

`bge768-wc64-r1` is an independent rebuild of the width-64 bed at the *same* seed.
It reproduces `bge768-ncomp64` to the digit — GET/query identical to two decimals
at all twelve nprobe points, recall within 0.0004:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| | `bge768-ncomp64` | `bge768-wc64-r1` (same seed, rebuilt)
| top-10 np25 | 0.9858 / 38.68 | 0.9860 / **38.68**
| top-20 np25 | 0.9835 / 39.00 | 0.9835 / **39.00**
|===

So rebuild-to-rebuild variance at a fixed seed is ~0 in requests and ≤0.0004 in
recall, while changing only the seed moves width-64's cost by **8.8%**. **The seed
effect is larger than the width effect it was being used to measure.** Every
per-width number in this TD carries that spread; none of them carry an error bar,
because until now there was no reason to think one was needed.

=== What survives, and what does not

The comparison against the *shipping* default reframes which claim was ever
load-bearing. At the default draw, 768-d:

[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| width | top-10 floor | top-20 floor
| **14 — shipping default** | np30 / 48.55 | np30 / 48.48
| 32 | np25 / 41.03 (−15.5%) | np30 / 48.01 (**−1.0%**)
| 64 | np25 / 38.68 (−20.3%) | np25 / 39.00 (−19.6%)
| 128 | np25 / 43.54 (−10.3%) | np25 / 43.04 (−11.2%)
|===

A **width-14 control at the alternate seed** — the shipping default itself, the
one bed the original campaign never built — was run to measure the title claim
directly rather than infer it. It is the row that settles the section:

.Alternate draw (seed 987654321), all three widths, same bed family
[cols="1,2,2", options="header"]
|===
| width | top-10 floor | top-20 floor
| **14 — shipping default** | np25 / 42.82 | np30 / 49.65
| 32 | np25 / **40.21** (−6.1%) | np30 / **47.18** (−5.0%)
| 64 | np25 / 42.08 (−1.7%) | np30 / 49.96 (**+0.6% — worse than the default**)
|===

* **Does not survive: the specific optimum.** "64 at 768-d" was read off a single
  draw at a resolution finer than the draw-to-draw spread. The headline −19.6% at
  top-20 came *entirely* from width 64 dropping a grid step — width 32 never
  bought more than 1% there — and that drop is exactly what failed to replicate.
  At the alternate draw width 64 is at parity with the shipping default and
  slightly *worse* at top-20.
* **Does not survive: the grid-step framing.** An earlier revision of this section
  claimed the floor drop from np30 to np25 survived as evidence of the defect.
  The width-14 control refutes it: at the alternate draw **all three widths floor
  at the same nprobe** (np25 at top-10, np30 at top-20), because width 14 *already*
  floors at np25 there. The floor position is set by the draw, not by the
  projection width, so it cannot be used as evidence for widening at all.
* **Does not survive: the 20–21% magnitude.** Measured against the shipping
  default at each draw, the best available width saves **20.3%/19.6%** at the
  default draw but only **6.1%/5.0%** at the alternate one — and the width that
  achieves it changes from 64 to 32.
* **Does not survive unqualified: the `k_c`-robustness result** (degrading `k_c`
  90→30 costs +21% at width 32 but only +2.6% at width 64). One draw per cell; it
  inherits the same doubt and is no longer sufficient to justify "width buys
  robustness" as a policy rationale.
* **Survives: widening helps, but only at width 32, and only by single digits at
  top-20.** Width 32 is the sole width measured that improves on the shipping
  default under **both** draws and **both** top-k — −15.5%/−1.0% at the default
  draw, −6.1%/−5.0% at the alternate one. Its sign is consistent even though its
  magnitude swings ~3×. Width 64's sign is not.
* **128-d BIGANN was never seed-replicated.** It is not refuted, but it rests on
  precisely the method that just failed at 768-d and must not be treated as
  settled.

=== Consequence for the flip

Setting a compiled default from per-width point estimates is not sound at this
resolution. To do it at all, the comparison needs **≥3 draws per width with the
spread reported**, and a decision rule over the distribution rather than a point —
a materially larger programme than the remaining flip preconditions assume.

The cheaper alternative that the surviving evidence does support: ship a
**conservative floor of 32** — the only width measured that improves on the
shipping default under every draw and every top-k, without claiming to be optimal.
It trades most of the headline saving for a result that does not depend on which
draw one measured. Expect **single-digit percent** at top-20, not 20%, and price
the +9…15% one-time write cost against that smaller number rather than against the
withdrawn one.

**Explicitly not recommended: a floor of 64.** It was the original recommendation,
and it is the width the control shows at parity-or-worse with the shipping default
at the alternate draw. Adopting it would pay the full write cost for a saving that
depends on which clustering draw a collection happens to land in.

== Resolution

The factorial called for below has now been run: 26 beds across **three**
dimensionalities, with two independently forced `k_c` values at 768-d. Two results
from it shape the policy.

**The optimum does not move with `k_c`.** Width 64 beat width 32 at both `k_c`=30
and `k_c`=90, so the policy needs **no `k_c` term** — which also means the `max()`
masking noted above is not the thing to fix first.

**Width buys robustness, and that is the decisive property.** [Superseded — this
rationale rests on one clustering draw per cell and does not survive
<<seed-replication>>; retained for the record.] Degrading `k_c`
90→30 costs **+21% GET/query at width 32 but only +2.6% at width 64**. Production
`k_c` is derived from corpus size (`(n_usable·dim)/4 MiB`, clamped 2..4096) and
therefore varies over orders of magnitude across collections and LSM levels, while
the width policy is a single constant serving all of them. A constant chosen at the
narrow end is fragile exactly where the geometry is least controlled.

So the resolution is a **floor**, not a formula:

. **`ncomp_floor` is plumbed and ships at 0** (legacy formula, bit-identical).
  Precedence: env `PROXIMADB_IVF_NCOMP_FLOOR` → TOML
  `[storage.sst_config.coarse_probe] ncomp_floor` → compiled-in default.
  `PROXIMADB_IVF_NCOMP` remains an absolute eval override so bench sweeps can still
  measure widths outside the policy band.
. **Do not fit another dimension-keyed model.** Four were fitted and refuted in
  turn (samples-per-parameter, fraction-of-dims, total-variance target, and a
  `4.9·dim^0.387` power law). The between-centroid-variance invariant is tight in
  *variance-share* space (93–97%) but **loose in width space** — T_b=92% predicts
  BIGANN at 24 against a measured 32, and T_b=96% predicts both BGE corpora at 128
  against a measured 64. No single threshold fits all three; it would be model #5.
  It is emitted as a diagnostic under `PROXIMADB_TRACE_IVF_FLUSH` so evidence keeps
  accruing without the policy depending on it.
. **Flip preconditions** — all four must pass before the compiled default moves:
  .. **Seed sensitivity — CHECKED 2026-08-22; FAILED.** Replicated the 32-vs-64
     ordering at a second `PROXIMADB_IVF_KMEANS_SEED`. **The ordering flips**: the
     top-20 grid-step advantage vanishes and the top-10 request ordering inverts,
     while a same-seed rebuild reproduces to the digit. Per the rule stated when
     this precondition was written, **nothing ships** on the strength of the
     per-width table. Full data: <<seed-replication>>. The remaining preconditions
     are moot until a distribution-based comparison replaces the point estimates.
  .. **Re-check the width optimum at the eligible read cap.** *Every width
     measurement in this TD was taken under the fixed 4 MiB planner* — the same
     conditioning the cell-size caveat below records for `k_c`. That is not a
     safe thing to assume away: TD-SEARCH-3 measured `k_c=30` at a 24 MiB cap
     near **17.9** wire GET/query against **49.5** at 4 MiB for ~4% more bytes.
     A swing of that size can move which projection width is optimal, because
     width and cap both act on how many cells must be probed to clear the recall
     floor. **The width optimum is therefore not established independently of the
     cap**, and if the 24 MiB default lands first, the 32-vs-64 comparison must be
     re-run under it before the floor is set from these numbers.
  .. **PCA conditioning at width — CHECKED; 64 clears it on every measured
     corpus.** The extractor uses power iteration with deflation from a fixed
     seed and no re-orthogonalisation, so past a spectrum-dependent depth it
     returns near-duplicates of components already extracted (filed as
     **TD-IVF-5**). Re-fitting it on the raw corpora puts first onset at component
     **111** for 128-d BIGANN and beyond 128 for both BGE corpora, so the ceiling
     sits at the measured optimum of **64** with margin. Adversarial synthetic
     spectra (`0.90^j`) breach it at 42, so the bound is corpus-dependent: a
     future corpus with much faster spectral decay needs the check re-run. A first
     pass that used only synthetic spectra wrongly concluded the cap had to be 32.
  .. **A0 size.** The directory carries `n_comp·dim + k_c·n_comp` f32s and is
     fetched whole on first touch. Guarded by a unit test asserting it stays
     within one 4 MiB IOP at the widest supported geometry (`k_c`=4096, dim=3072).
. **Per-collection surface stays deferred.** The optimum is `k_c`-independent and
  moves only between 32 and 64 across three very different corpora, so the knob
  would be exercised almost never; the TOML mirror covers the operator escape
  without a catalog-schema change.

**Mixed-read safety is already satisfied.** `n_comp` is persisted per segment in
the A0 coarse directory and read back from the file, so existing segments retain
their stored width and only newly written segments adopt a new default. No
flag-day, per the storage-format migration mandate.

== Cost model (both dimensionalities, clean serial measurements)

....
  128-d, 3.3M rows:  write_ms =  79,828 + 521.3 * n_comp   R^2=0.997
  768-d, 491k rows:  write_ms = 274,711 + 502.1 * n_comp   R^2=0.9997
....

The **slopes match (521 vs 502 ms/dim) despite 6.7× fewer rows**.

[WARNING]
====
**CORRECTED (2026-08-14). The inference below this line was wrong, and it was
load-bearing — it is the reason this TD treated a widened default as
cost-free at scale.**

The original argument ran: "if the per-dim term were projecting and assigning all
rows, the 768-d slope would be ~78 ms/dim; it is not, so the per-dimension cost is
k-means over the training sample, and is therefore independent of collection size
— it amortises away entirely as collections grow."

That drops a factor. The row-linear work is `N·dim` per component, not `N`. The two
beds are **8.45e8 (3.3M × 128) vs 7.55e8 (491k × 768)** — **12% apart, not 6.7×**.
Fewer rows and more dimensions very nearly cancel, so matching slopes is exactly
what a row-linear term predicts. The measurement does not discriminate between the
two hypotheses at all; the beds are confounded.

Decomposing the actual algorithm, at `n_train`≈50k and `k_c`≈90–100, the
**N-scaling terms (project-all-rows + assign-all-rows) are ~75–78% of the
per-component slope**, and the sample-scaling k-means terms are the remainder. If
that holds, write cost scales with `N` and **does not amortise**: +14% at 3.3M is
still +14% at 100M.

**Status: not yet settled by measurement.** Two cheap ways to settle it, both
using gates that already exist:

. Fix `PROXIMADB_IVF_K` and vary `N` by 4× at fixed `dim`. A slope that scales with
  `N` is the projection term.
. Read it straight off the flush trace. `PROXIMADB_TRACE_IVF_FLUSH` now reports
  `project(sample)` and `project+assign` as separate phases precisely so the
  sample-scaling and N-scaling halves are directly comparable.

Mitigation already landed: the in-memory path used to project every row **twice**
(once to build a full coordinate buffer, once inside `classify`), while holding an
`O(N·n_comp)` buffer whose only surviving use was PC1. Both are removed, so the
N-scaling half of the slope is now roughly half what it was measured at — which
lowers the real cost of widening regardless of how the attribution resolves.
====

CAUTION: timings require a quiet machine. A first attempt at the 768-d costs
produced 334k/468k/307k ms — non-monotonic, despite identical compaction paths —
because the host slept and ran analysis concurrently. The numbers above use
`caffeinate -i`, serial execution, and the minimum of two builds per width
(contention only ever inflates a timer). Recall and object-store GET/query are
contention-invariant; **no timing is**.

== Determinism, and what is therefore NOT a risk

`PAX_IVF_KMEANS_SEED` is a fixed constant and `block_cluster.rs:737` documents the
pipeline as "deterministic end to end: strided sample-train, seeded k-means,
fixed-init". An independent rebuild of the 768-d `n_comp=64` bed reproduced
**recall to sd 0.00013 and GET/query to 0.000** across 24 points, with identical
`coarse_seed` and `cell_row_max_to_mean`.

Consequently width comparisons carry **no bed-construction noise** and small
deltas are real signal. An earlier revision of this TD quoted σ = 0.0027 as a
bed-to-bed noise floor; that figure came from a run whose training-sample size
also differed, so it measured the training-size effect and mislabelled it noise.
All conclusions that were hedged against it stand unhedged.

RESIDUAL uncertainty is different in kind: every result is **one clustering draw**
from a hard-coded seed. Sensitivity to initialisation is unmeasured and needs the
seed made overridable. Determinism was also verified on one machine — k-means
parallel reduction order may vary with thread count, so CI on other hardware may
not reproduce bit-identically.

== Reconciling with TD-WLP-4b

`block_cluster.rs` :294 records _"Phase-0 sweep: recall@10 flat 0.989 for n_comp ∈
[4,128]"_. **Not contradicted, and independently reproduced here**: at 768-d and
nprobe 40–50 the 14-component bed slightly *beats* the 32-component one (0.9941 vs
0.9934). Both hold:

* **n_comp does not change maximum achievable recall** — TD-WLP-4b, correct.
* **n_comp changes the recall-per-probe frontier** — this TD.

A sweep at generous `nprobe` measures the first and cannot see the second.

== Gate (prerequisite)

No CI gate observes probe economy. `sift_pax_recall_ratchet_test` (qa-gate, WS8)
asserts recall@10 ≥ 0.90 on SIFT1M — every width in this study clears that by a
wide margin while differing 21% in GET/query. A separate regression (the pre-#1422
spill path) silently moved 10M GET/query ~46% with nothing catching it.

Proposed: a deterministic 1M bed (k_c=30; 100K is unusable, its floor equals `k_c`
so there is no headroom), ratcheted **at a fixed nprobe**:

* `recall@10 ≥ baseline` and `GET/query ≤ baseline × 1.05`

Fixed nprobe rather than the floor — but not because of measurement noise, which
we now know is ~0. The floor **quantises a continuous curve**, so an arbitrarily
small real change becomes a whole-grid-step regression; a fixed-nprobe assertion
tracks the same underlying quantity and degrades gracefully. The ×1.05 tolerance
exists to absorb CI hardware/threading differences, not scatter.

== Adjacent findings (measured, not proposed here)

* **The 4MiB-conditioned geometry is bracketed, but 4 MiB is not vindicated as
  a provider optimum.** Under the fixed 4 MiB planner, GET/query is U-shaped in
  `k_c` with its local minimum at the natural value, while bytes/query fall
  monotonically as cells get finer:
+
....
   k_c  MiB/cell  floor@10   GET/q   MB/q   %corpus touched
    30      12.0        12    49.5    170        40.0%
    90       4.0        25    41.0    116        27.8%   <- 4MiB-cap local minimum
   300       1.2        60    57.4     93        20.0%
....
+
Coarser was already known to lose (a `k_c=50`/8MiB test at 128-d cost +7.2% GETs
at iso-recall). Finer loses too: more probes cost more round-trips than the byte
saving is worth. Because Hot bills requests and Cool bills bytes, the two tiers
want different geometries — see link:TD-COST-1-cold-tier-posture-unsafe-for-query-serving-collections.adoc[TD-COST-1].


* **The cell-size conclusion is conditional on the 4 MiB read cap.** Forcing
  `k_c=50` at 3.3M (approximately 8 MiB cells) measured **+7.2% application
  GETs at iso-recall**, +21% bytes and +11% write under the fixed 4 MiB planner.
  But flat Azure Blob has no 4 MiB billing quantum, and the current application
  counter sits above the SDK. On the 768-d `k_c=30`, width-32 bed, nprobe 12
  measured 49.542 application GET/query while Region B alone read 150.30 MB;
  the approximately 12.5 MiB average cell is mechanically split by the planner.
  TD-SEARCH-3's fixed-bed wire sweep subsequently measured `k_c=30` at 24 MiB
  near 17.9 wire GET/query versus 49.5 at 4 MiB, with only approximately 4%
  more bytes and approximately 6% more RSS. That result is not directly
  comparable to `k_c=90` at 4 MiB: cap and geometry changed together. A
  `k_c=90` rerun at the same eligible cap is required before this TD calls any
  cell size Hot-Blob GET-optimal. `k_c=200` remains useful afterward for the
  independent byte/selectivity trade-off. The 24 MiB default remains blocked
  on TD-SEARCH-3's exact-current paired non-inferiority rerun.
* **Read metering, verified against vendor docs.** Azure **Blob** bills one
  transaction per request with **no 4 MiB increment** — that rule is ADLS Gen2
  (`dfs.`) only, and ADR-036 puts us on the flat Blob endpoint. S3 and GCS also
  bill per request. All three charge per-GB retrieval **only** on cold tiers
  (Hot/Standard are free). Thus GET/query is the direct *object-store request
  COGS* metric for hot-tier collections; it is not the complete read-cost or
  performance metric. Bytes still affect latency, cache capacity, Cool/Cold
  retrieval, and the separately metered network/egress dimension. The 4MiB target
  is not a billing quantum — it needs a latency/cache/memory justification instead.
* **A larger prize may sit next door.** Because no cloud penalises request *size*,
  coalescing region A (≈551 KiB/cell of codes) with region B (≈3.72 MiB/cell of
  rerank vectors) into one ranged read could take GET/query from ~26 to ~13 —
  bigger than this entire TD. It forfeits the two-stage filter, so it is **free on
  Hot and costs per-GB retrieval on Cool/Cold**: tier-conditional, and worth a
  design pass before implementation.
