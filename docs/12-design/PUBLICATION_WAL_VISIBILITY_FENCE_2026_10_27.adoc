= The Shared Publication/WAL Visibility Fence (P2 delete writer, contract)

Status: **Proposed (2026-10-27)** — the contract the live incremental delete writer must
satisfy before any reader-side refusal in its name is relaxed. Written down first, per the
P2 pickup's instruction that unordered-delta refusal stands "until a shared visibility fence
is proven."

== The two views

A published table has TWO read surfaces that must agree on which rows are dead:

[cols="2,3,3"]
|===
| | Native/WAL view | Published (Iceberg) view
| Authority | the WAL: tombstones + memtable merge keyed by oid (`valid_to_ns == Some(0)` or TTL-expired) | the snapshot's data files minus each file's deletion vectors (positions)
| Served by | pgwire/native scans (`read_changes_since` delta-merge over `snapshot_lsn`) | the DataFusion/duckdb readers (`object_store_parquet_reader`), external engines
| Key | canonical **oid** | per-file **row position**
| Dead when | a tombstone for the oid is at or before the read's LSN | the row's position is in its file's DV, in the snapshot being read
|===

The bridge between the keys is `published_positions_of` (merged #2262): oid to (file,
position). The bridge between the views is this contract.

== The invariant

FENCE (single statement). For every row R of a published table T killed by a **fenced
delete** (an explicit tombstone), and every snapshot S of T committed at or after a point
in time F, all reads at or after F observe the same liveness for R from both views: R is
hidden from BOTH the WAL merge and the published snapshot, or from NEITHER — or a view
whose delta ordering against the fence is unknown REFUSES rather than guessing.

Two scoping notes, stated up front so no gate author chases a counterexample:

* **TTL deaths are NOT fenced.** The published snapshot carries no `valid_to_ns` — TTL on
  the base is eventual-until-next-publication (a documented first-cut limitation of the
  delta merge). A TTL expiry therefore disagrees between the views until the next full
  publication, and no fence can change that; the invariant covers explicit tombstones
  only.
* **"Agree or refuse."** The refusal (a nonempty WAL delta that cannot be ordered
  against a DV-carrying snapshot) is a legitimate outcome, not a violation: the reader
  says "I cannot order this" instead of guessing. The invariant permits hidden-in-both,
  live-in-both, or refusal — never hidden-here-live-there for a tombstoned row.

A delete that has not yet crossed the fence may be visible in one view and not the other —
that window is the *fence gap*, and the contract's job is to bound it, not to deny it.

== The ordering (the fence's mechanism)

A live DELETE runs in four phases. The fence is the boundary between phase 2 and phase 3;
it is a *point in the publication ordering*, not a new lock class.

[cols="1,4,3"]
|===
| Phase | Action | Failure mode
| 1. Resolve | op-locks + DML locks taken (today's `execute_delete_rows` path); oids resolved; `capture_snapshot_lsn` reads the WAL's current LSN L | as today
| 2. WAL commit | tombstones appended at LSNs > L; the WAL now hides the oids | as today (WAL-authoritative)
| 3. **FENCE** | under the table's `publish_lock` (per tenant+table, in-process) + `PublishFence::check` (write lease): map oids to positions (`published_positions_of`; never-published oids are benign — the row is WAL-only, no DV needed — while a file written before the hidden oid column makes mapping impossible and the fence REFUSES), write each file's **complete** deletion set (the parent's DVs ∪ the new positions — a writer that publishes only its own positions resurrects every previously deleted row), publish the successor snapshot S-prime chaining onto the CURRENT published parent, recording as `snapshot_lsn` a value **at or above every tombstone LSN published** (a fresh capture after phase 2, or the max tombstone LSN) | see Crash and Conflict below
| 4. Release | publish lock and fence released; S-prime is the new published truth | —
|===

The recorded `snapshot_lsn` is load-bearing, not bookkeeping: at or above every published
tombstone, the OLAP delta-merge sees an EMPTY changed-set and serves the DV-carrying base
directly (the row is hidden through the fence); below them, the changed-set is non-empty
against a DV-carrying base and the reader REFUSES — gate 1 then tests the refusal, not
the hide, and the unordered-delta relaxation condition (suppress-set covers every DV
tombstone) is unstateable. The value is part of the fence, not an implementation detail.

Completeness precondition (writer slice, round 2): an EMPTY changed-set at the recorded
LSN is only correct when the successor IS a complete state at that LSN. The live publisher
writes no data files — its base is the parent's files (the state at the parent's capture
LSN) minus the DVs — so it must REFUSE unless (a) the parent is the publication the layout
recorded (an externally published parent's capture LSN is unknown), and (b) the changed-set
since the parent's recorded `snapshot_lsn` is exactly the tombstones this delete is
publishing. Any other changed oid is in neither the parent's files nor the replayed delta:
publishing would hide it from BOTH views. A refused fence leaves the standing gap (WAL
dead, published stale) until the next full publication reconciles.

Ordering rule: phase 3 publishes ONLY oids whose tombstones are already durable (phase 2
completed). A reader that sees snapshot S-prime and then reads the WAL at LSN at or above
its tombstone LSNs sees the row dead in both. A reader that reads the WAL first (row dead)
and then S-prime (row still live in an older snapshot — one from before the fence) sees
the row dead in the newer WAL view; that is correct WAL semantics (the WAL is the fresher
source), and the published view converges at the next publication.

== Why this is a fence, not a distributed transaction

The two stores commit independently (WAL append; metadata CAS). Full atomicity across them
would require 2PC — rejected: for WAL-routed writes the WAL is the authority and the
published view is a *projection* whose staleness is bounded by the publication interval
(ADR-094's seam; strictly, ADR-094's Decision 1 is "format follows modality" — the
WAL-authority premise is the seam's, not a numbered decision).

**A second publisher exists and the contract must name it.** The bulk-append lane
(`WriteLane::BulkAppendCommit`, `src/query/table_write_executor.rs`) publishes successors
on the same metadata log while holding NEITHER the publish lock NOR a fence, and its rows
bypass the WAL entirely (parts + Iceberg commit). The sole-authority premise is therefore
**scoped to WAL-routed writes**: bulk-appended rows never enter the delete's phase-1
resolution (their oids are not in the WAL) and cannot be fenced by this contract. Chain
safety against that racer rests on the successor CAS alone (both publishers retry from the
new parent and re-map), which is why the CAS — not the lease — is the fork barrier; the
lease fence additionally keeps a displaced POD from publishing. A later slice either
forbids the bulk lane for fenced tables or extends the fence to it; the writer TD must
pick one before activation.

The fence therefore guarantees:

1. **No resurrection across the fence.** Once S-prime commits, no later snapshot may name
   the deleted rows' positions as live. Mechanism, split by successor kind:
   * **Successors that CARRY files** (append, and the fenced writer itself): the DVs ride
     forward — the parent's full per-file sets, unioned with any new positions.
   * **A full REWRITE** (which re-writes rows at NEW positions, so carried DVs do not
     apply): the existing entry guard (`dml/mod.rs` ~2876) refuses ANY publication from a
     DV-carrying parent — appends included, not just rewrites. The ONLY safe relaxation,
     taken by the writer slice itself, is a rewrite whose capture LSN is **at or above
     the DV parent's recorded `snapshot_lsn`** (every DV'd oid's tombstone is visible in
     the native read, so the rewritten parts cannot contain it). Below that LSN the
     rewrite is a resurrection window and the guard STANDS. The unfenced MATERIALIZE path
     never inherits; only the fenced writer may, under this condition.
2. **No fork-before-commit.** Phase 3 runs under the per-(tenant, table) `publish_lock`
   (in-process serialization) AND `PublishFence::check` (the write-lease authority,
   `LeasePublishFence`): a pod that lost the lease cannot publish S-prime, so two pods
   cannot fork the snapshot chain from the same parent — and the successor-slot CAS is
   the cross-process barrier that also covers the lock-free bulk-append lane. Losing the
   CAS race (`AlreadyExists` on the successor slot) is a retry from the new parent,
   re-mapping positions (positions are FILE-relative; the file set is parent-dependent).
3. **Convergence bound.** Between phase 2 and phase 3 the views disagree (WAL dead,
   published live). This is the fence gap. It is bounded by the publication latency, and
   it closes monotonically: no interleaving can re-widen it once phase 3 commits, because
   (1) holds.

== What each reader may assume (and may not)

* **WAL readers** (pgwire native, delta-merge): unchanged. The WAL is authoritative; a
  tombstone hides the row regardless of any snapshot. The delta-merge already suppresses
  published rows whose oids are tombstoned — that mechanism IS the WAL-side half of the
  fence and needs no new code.
* **Published readers** (DataFusion table provider, duckdb view, external engines): read
  a snapshot with its DVs applied. They may see the fence gap (a row the WAL already
  killed, still live in an older snapshot) — that is snapshot semantics, not corruption.
  They may NEVER see a row that the fence has crossed reappear: guaranteed by (1).
* **The unordered-delta refusal, and its one relaxation (the ordered DV base, 2026-10-29).**
  The OLAP merge refuses a non-empty delta against a DV-carrying base (the reader guards
  from #2256) UNLESS the base IS the publication the catalog layout recorded: opened
  through the snapshot manifest at exactly the layout's `iceberg_metadata_version`.
  Why that is sufficient:
  ** Only ProximaDB's own publishers write the recorded `(iceberg_metadata_version,
     snapshot_lsn)` pair: MATERIALIZE's flip, `advance_snapshot_lsn`, the live-delete
     flip (and the debug-only gate-3 hook, which records its parent's LSN). DDL cannot
     set it.
  ** Each of them records a base complete apart from the delta at its LSN (MATERIALIZE by
     construction; the live publisher by the completeness precondition; the advance only
     when nothing changed), and every vector in it is backed by a WAL-durable death.
  ** So the merge — base minus vectors, minus every changed oid, plus the current rows of
     the changed oids — applies exactly the tombstone set the publication applied. That
     is the condition this contract set for relaxing the refusal.
  ** Everything else still refuses: an external DV commit or a crash-gap orphan (the
     latest version moved past the recorded one), a listed or cached open (no proven
     version), a layout with no recorded version, and a decoy layout (the version is
     bound to the layout NAMED `parquet-snapshot` at the location being opened).
     Metadata versions only grow, so a publication racing a read makes the opened
     version newer than the recorded one: a refusal, never a wrong row.
  ** Kill switch: `PROXIMADB_OLAP_ORDERED_DV_BASE=0` restores the unconditional refusal.
     With incremental MATERIALIZE ON (the default), turning ONLY this off is an
     outage: every table that took an incremental publication carries `dv-mat`
     vectors, so every OLAP read with a nonempty delta refuses (`db error`) until
     the next MATERIALIZE — and that MATERIALIZE stays incremental, so the
     refusal returns with the next write. Disarm both together: set
     `PROXIMADB_MATERIALIZE_INCREMENTAL_DV=0`, then re-MATERIALIZE each affected
     table (the entry guard admits its own `dv-mat` parent and the rewrite drops
     the vectors).

== Crash and conflict

* **Crash after phase 2, before phase 3:** the WAL is dead-authoritative; the published
  view is stale within the fence gap. Recovery = the next publication (MATERIALIZE or a
  later delete) re-crosses the fence. No data loss; bounded staleness.
* **Crash mid-phase 3 (DV written, CAS lost or pod died):** the Puffin file is an
  orphan (`put_if_absent`, never referenced by any manifest). Harmless; a GC follow-up.
  The successor slot CAS is the atomic commit — if it did not land, nothing is published.
* **Crash AFTER the CAS but before the catalog layout flip:** S-prime IS published (the
  metadata log names it) while the layout still names the older version. Recovery is the
  LAYOUT FLIP (the next publication or the next plan_append observation), not a
  re-publication; until it flips, appends classify the layout as stale and fall to
  rewrite, which the entry guard then gates on the DV condition above.
* **The bulk-append lane (`WriteLane::BulkAppendCommit`):** FORBIDDEN above a
  DV-carrying parent (the writer slice's forbid-or-fence decision). That lane
  commits a successor without the `publish_lock` and never records the catalog
  layout flip, so running it above a DV parent strands every later
  publication's (metadata version, snapshot LSN) pairing — both the guard's
  relaxation and the publisher's completeness precondition would refuse
  forever, and after any later WAL delete every read of a nonempty delta
  against the DV base refuses. The DML routing reads the parent's DV state
  BEFORE the lane is chosen (a commit-time refusal would strand the written
  parts) and demotes the statement to the WAL lane. Since incremental
  MATERIALIZE (which publishes vectors and does NOT take the DML lock) is ON by
  default, that read is repeated under the table's `publish_lock` once the bulk
  lane is chosen, and the lock is held through the bulk commit: in one process
  no publisher can commit vectors between the eligibility read and the bulk
  commit (publishers in another process remain gate 3's CAS/lease fence). The
  lock is keyed on the RESOLVED table identity, not the SQL spelling. Cost: a
  bulk append waits for an in-flight MATERIALIZE (O(table)) and vice versa; the
  DML lease (10 s, not renewed) can lapse during that wait, which is safe
  because correctness rests on the publish lock, and a long wait raises the
  chance a MATERIALIZE's lease fence refuses at commit (retry). A statement
  demoted to the WAL lane releases the lock. The bulk
  lane's snapshot
  ids join MATERIALIZE's millisecond scale, bumped past the parent's, so the
  lane can no longer starve later publications of valid ids.
* **Concurrent publisher (MATERIALIZE racing a DELETE):** both hold the `publish_lock`;
  the loser retries against the new parent. If MATERIALIZE wins, the delete re-maps
  positions against the NEW file set (its rows may now live in rewritten files) and
  re-crosses the fence. If the delete wins, MATERIALIZE's plan_append sees a
  DV-carrying parent and falls to its existing rewrite path, whose entry guard refuses —
  the writer must instead make the rewrite path *inherit* the DVs (carry-through), which
  is the same inheritance as (1).
* **The lease fence:** `PublishFence::check` immediately precedes the CAS, as
  `materialize_table_to_parquet_fenced` already does for full publications. A displaced
  pod fails the check and publishes nothing.

== What this contract deliberately does not decide

* Whether the delete's phase 3 publishes synchronously in the DELETE round-trip or
  asynchronously after the WAL commit (the fence permits both, with the lock lifetimes
  above; the choice is latency vs round-trip budget, decided at implementation).
* **UPDATE publication** (the ladder's step 3 covers "incremental update/delete"): an
  UPDATE is delete+insert of a NEW oid, so its phase 3 must publish the DV *and* the
  appended rows — the four phases model the delete half only. Scoped as the writer
  slice's follow-on, not a hidden assumption.
* The Iceberg REST catalog's and external engines' own snapshot caches (beyond the
  in-repo DataFusion table cache already deferred): out of scope here.
* Multi-pod cache coherence of the published view (the DataFusion table cache): out of
  scope here; the snapshot cache bypasses from #2256 remain.
* The footer/sidecar oid-to-position index that removes the O(published bytes) mapping
  cost (the #2262 cost statement names it the follow-up).

== Proving it (the acceptance gates)

The writer slice is DONE only when all of these hold, tested through the real pgwire
facade (not test-side DV construction):

1. **Delete, both views agree:** DELETE over pgwire against a materialized table; the
   row is hidden from a pgwire SELECT (this leg is phase-2 evidence — the WAL hides the
   row regardless of the fence) and from an external reader (the fence-specific leg: the
   DV-carrying S-prime served bare), after the fence crosses, in one test.
2. **No resurrection across snapshots:** publish S-prime (the delete), then append and
   publish S-double-prime; the deleted row is still hidden in S-double-prime (DV
   inheritance).
3. **Fence conflict:** a concurrent MATERIALIZE racing the delete; the loser re-maps and
   both views still agree. Forcing mechanism (without it GREEN is vacuous): a test hook
   that holds the publish lock across a barrier — the delete blocks in phase 3, the test
   runs the MATERIALIZE, the lock releases, and the delete's CAS retry re-maps against
   the new parent.
4. **Lease fence:** the publish fails closed when the lease check fails — tested at the
   `PublishFence` seam with a fence whose `check` errs (real lease displacement cannot
   be forced single-pod; the seam is the contract's surface, embedded deployments run
   `NoPublishFence` by design).
5. **Crash gap:** kill after the WAL commit before publication (a fault-injection point
   between phases 2 and 3, on the pattern of the existing
   `PROXIMADB_TEST_TXN_COMMIT_FAULT` hook); the WAL view is dead, the published view is
   stale-but-consistent, and the next publication converges.

Lock lifetimes (the sync/async choice must state these): synchronously, the DML and
op-locks are held through phase 4 — correct, at the cost of serializing table DML for
the publication latency; asynchronously, they are released after phase 2 and the
publication carries only the publish lock and fence — but then a concurrent DML's own
publication must chain via the same CAS-retry, never assume it is the only publisher.
The writer slice picks one and states it; both are conforming.
6. **The reader guards stand:** the unordered-delta refusal and snapshot cache bypasses
   remain in force and tested.

=== Gate status (2026-10-28, the gates slice)

All six are implemented and pinned by e2e through the real pgwire facade
(`tests/iceberg_interop_e2e.rs`, publisher armed in isolated children):

1. DONE — `live_delete_publishes_and_both_views_hide_the_row` (artifact
   assertions prove the publication fired; the external leg is CI-required).
2. DONE — `live_delete_survives_a_successor_publication` (both the WAL and
   the published view).
3. DONE — `live_delete_loses_a_cas_race_and_the_retry_closes_the_fence`. The
   forcing mechanism is a DETERMINISTIC competitor injection
   (`PROXIMADB_TEST_FENCE_INJECT_COMPETITOR`), not a lock barrier: the hook
   commits a legitimate no-change successor from the same parent and flips
   the layout, so the real CAS loses and the bounded retry re-reads, re-maps,
   and re-unions against the new parent — the same interleaving the barrier
   forces, without sleeps or true concurrency.
4. DONE — the publisher consults the shared `WriteLeaseAuthority` (the same
   value DDL consults) twice per attempt: before any publication I/O (so a
   displaced pod orphans nothing) and immediately before the CAS (the check is
   point-in-time, so this bounds the displaced-pod window); unit tests pin
   allow/inert/misrouted at the authority (real displacement cannot be forced
   single-pod; the seam is the contract's surface).
5. DONE — `crash_gap_is_fail_closed_and_materialize_adopts`
   (`PROXIMADB_TEST_FENCE_CRASH_BEFORE_FLIP` skips the flip after the CAS):
   the wedge refuses reads fail-closed, and the MATERIALIZE guard ADOPTS the
   orphaned publication — its `dv-live-*` Puffin proves it is ours and the
   phase ordering proves its tombstones durable — healing the table.
6. DONE, then RELAXED (2026-10-29, the ordered DV base) —
   `ordered_dv_base_serves_the_merge_across_the_fence`: a post-fence WAL write
   (an insert, then a delete of a base row) is MERGED over the DV base because
   the base is the recorded publication; `ordered_dv_base_kill_switch_restores_the_refusal`
   pins the original refusal under `PROXIMADB_OLAP_ORDERED_DV_BASE=0`, and the
   external-DV and crash-gap cases (gate 5) still refuse.

The activation flip of `PROXIMADB_LIVE_DELETE_PUBLISHER` remains gated on
these six plus soak evidence; the knob stays default-OFF.

=== MATERIALIZE as a fenced carry-through writer (2026-10-29)

With `PROXIMADB_MATERIALIZE_INCREMENTAL_DV` (default ON since 2026-10-29), MATERIALIZE itself publishes updates
and deletes incrementally, and it becomes the second writer of DV-bearing recorded
versions. It upholds the writer contract (`recorded_publication`): its successor is
complete by construction at the captured LSN. Every oid changed since the parent's
LSN is either re-appended as its live after-image or has its old positions hidden;
an oid unchanged since then is exact in the parent's files; one changed again after
the capture is in the reader's delta. Every vector it mints (`dv-mat-*`) hides only
a superseded or WAL-deleted row version. Guarantee 1 holds per file: a touched
file's single vector is the parent's union the new positions, and untouched parent
vectors are carried by reference. The crash-gap adoption recognizes `dv-mat-*` as
self-minted, by file name, ungated.

=== Soak evidence (2026-10-28)

`soak_mixed_workload_holds_the_fence` runs a seeded mixed workload with the
publisher armed. **Correctness holds:** the WAL view matches the model after
every round, and the OLAP view reconciles to the model at every MATERIALIZE.
**Reach is narrow:** under interleaved inserts the completeness precondition
refuses every DV-only publication (45 deletes, 0 publications). A pending
insert is in neither the parent's files nor a vector, so publishing would hide
it from both views. In the eligible shape (deletes as the only writes since the
last publication) every delete publishes (one Puffin and one metadata version
each) and the OLAP view tracks it with no MATERIALIZE. An insert on top of the
DV-carrying base then makes the OLAP read refuse the unordered delta, and a
MATERIALIZE over the DV parent reconciles both views. The reach question is filed as TD-LIVEDEL-1 and is part of the
activation decision.

=== Incremental MATERIALIZE soak (2026-10-29)

`soak_incremental_materialize_holds_both_views` runs 40 seeded rounds of
inserts, updates and deletes with `PROXIMADB_MATERIALIZE_INCREMENTAL_DV` on and
a MATERIALIZE every third round. After every round the WAL view equals the
model, and the OLAP view's count, key sum and updated-value count equal it,
both between publications (merged over the DV-carrying base) and after them.
Deltas under the 20% bound publish incrementally; deltas past it compact.
Superseded `dv-mat-*` Puffins are collected by the generation GC (bounded by
the parts the current and previous snapshots name); the final state reads back
through the external engines in CI (#2286). Run with CI's pinned readers
required: 41 updates, 20 deletes, 15 MATERIALIZEs, at most 2 `dv-mat` Puffins on
disk, pyiceberg (REST and static) and DuckDB exact. On that evidence the gate is
ON by default from 2026-10-29; `PROXIMADB_MATERIALIZE_INCREMENTAL_DV=0` is the
kill switch back to full rewrites.
