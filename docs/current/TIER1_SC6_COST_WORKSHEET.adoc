== What is measured today (inputs, not conclusions)

Request-column entries from the E1 sweeps (n=1–2 per cell; directions, not
distributions; production S3 already targets 8 MiB ranges, so the 16 MiB
column is the candidate, not a regression):

[cols="1,2,2,2",options="header"]
|===
| Corpus (shape) | Requests/q at cap 4 MiB | Requests/q at cap 16 MiB | Delta
| 1M, single segment | baseline | −28.67% requests, +4.50% bytes | candidate
| 3M, single segment | 36.342 | 23.634 (−34.97%), one fewer wave | candidate
| 10M, 3-segment bed | baseline | −5.54% (segment fan-out dominates) | weak
| Fragmented (1→4 segs, 3M) | — | cap lever collapses at first fragmentation (−33% → −13/−16% plateau) | bounded
|===

Read-model terms, per the design: the persistent store serves whole-object
reads for a granted collection (SC4), so the *object request* column above
is the COLD/balanced term; a granted collection's steady-state payload GETs
are zero by the SC4 gate (control requests are metered separately). Bytes/q
and p50/p99 for both arms come from the same runs.

== Competitor normalization (structure only)

Compare at equal: region, dimension/precision, keys/attributes, filters,
recall target, freshness, QPS/concurrency, result bytes, availability,
churn, namespace size. Logical vendor bytes ≠ our compressed physical
traffic; account for query fan-out and request minimums (sharding to exploit
a floor may raise total cost). Vendors in scope per the design: S3 Vectors,
turbopuffer. Each column names its source (vendor list price / our run) and
its date. Finance owns the final comparison; this worksheet only fixes the
columns and the measurement protocol.

== Explicit boundaries

* No price is quoted here; rate cards are AnvaiOps.
* No default flip (range cap, residency, tier) is claimed; every default
  change needs the soak in the rollout runbook.
* n=1–2 cells do not establish optima; the fragmented/filtered finalists
  remain the design's gate for any global range-cap change.
