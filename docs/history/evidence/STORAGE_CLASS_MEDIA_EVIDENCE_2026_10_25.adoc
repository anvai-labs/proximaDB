== Local direct-I/O diagnostic: measured

A task-owned 1 GiB file, guest O_DIRECT, aligned mmap buffers, 4/8/16/32/40/64 MiB
reads, queue depths 1 and 8, contiguous or seeded shuffled offsets. The file was created
with O_EXCL and removed after the run. No global cache drop or other workload changes.
At QD8, the contiguous arm distributes consecutive extents across workers; actual device
completion order is not guaranteed sequential. Wall throughput includes scheduling.

Selected shuffled-offset results:

[cols="1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Queue depth | Read MiB | MiB/s | Median read ms
| 1 | 8 | 4,273 | 1.76
| 1 | 40 | 4,794 | 7.52
| 8 | 8 | 6,348 | 9.12
| 8 | 40 | 6,480 | 41.09
|===

At QD1, 40 MiB gives about 12% more bandwidth with 4.27x median latency; at QD8,
about 2% more bandwidth with 4.51x latency. This compares per-read latency for five
times the bytes, not equal-work query completion. It does not prove 8 MiB is globally
optimal. At QD1, adjacent 8/40 MiB reads delivered 4,293/5,143 MiB/s respectively.

Limitations: Windows/VHD caching is uncontrolled despite guest direct I/O, 1 GiB is small
relative to host RAM, trials share the file, and background workloads were not isolated.
Reported raw p95 values have only 16–64 samples and are diagnostic, not tail-SLO evidence.
Do not label the results raw-device sustained bandwidth or cold-NVMe performance.

== Local S3-compatible MinIO: measured

Used existing MinIO `127.0.0.1:12000`; existing Toxiproxy `:12002` adds 5 ms upstream
and 5 ms downstream latency, zero configured jitter. Proxy settings were read, not changed.
Created a unique task-owned bucket with one 256 MiB object using multipart upload;
deleted the experiment object and bucket on completion. Credentials were consumed only
in memory from the selected local MinIO service, not logged or saved.

Each arm reads eight seeded noncontiguous ranges; response 206 and exact byte count are
checked. Boto3 drains the body in 1 MiB chunks. QD8 is a single batch of eight requests,
not a sustained load test. The object and host caches are warm. These results include
HTTP/client/proxy overhead but no ANN selection, recall, coalescing or decode.

[cols="2,1,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Endpoint | QD | Read MiB | MiB/s | Median read ms
| Direct | 1 | 8 | 1,091 | 7.30
| Direct | 1 | 40 | 1,438 | 27.77
| Proxy 5+5 ms | 1 | 8 | 383 | 20.79
| Proxy 5+5 ms | 1 | 40 | 893 | 44.76
| Direct | 8 | 8 | 2,627 | 18.14
| Direct | 8 | 40 | 4,237 | 69.60
| Proxy 5+5 ms | 8 | 8 | 1,897 | 28.81
| Proxy 5+5 ms | 8 | 40 | 2,524 | 110.46
|===

Inference: larger useful transfers amortize fixed request overhead, especially serially.
Concurrent smaller reads already hide some of it. These runs always requested the full
range; they do not establish that an ANN query has 40 MiB of useful contiguous bytes.
With sparse hits, widening reads may increase cost and latency despite higher MiB/s.
MinIO validates S3-shaped range behavior, not EBS seek time, credits, AWS billing,
durability, access-tier enforcement or separate-host contention. ds3 was not used.

== Interpolation and EBS extrapolation: derived, not measured

Within the proxy QD1 sample, a simple line through the measured 8 and 40 MiB medians is:

----
T_ms(size_MiB) ~= 14.80 + 0.7491 * size_MiB
----

It predicts 26.78 ms at 16 MiB versus measured 26.27, and 38.77 at 32 versus 39.37.
At 64 MiB it predicts 62.74 versus measured 63.48. This is an in-environment sensitivity
fit, not a statistical confidence interval or an EBS latency model. Intercept includes
client/proxy/service overhead and must not be called measured CPU time or pure RTT.
Larger sizes, sustained concurrency and different hardware need new measurements.

For EBS, replace the media service constraint, not the entire application latency with
a single NVMe multiplier. A transfer lower bound is `1000 * size_MiB / bandwidth_MiBps`.
Assume sufficient EC2 EBS bandwidth, no competing traffic, full useful reads and no
CPU/IOPS/seek bottleneck. HDD rows use credit-depleted 1 TiB baselines:

[cols="2,1,1,1",options="header"]
|===
| Scenario | MiB/s | 8 MiB transfer floor | 40 MiB transfer floor
| gp3 included baseline | 125 | 64 ms | 320 ms
| gp3 provisioned-throughput scenario | 500 | 16 ms | 80 ms
| gp2 1 TiB throughput ceiling | 250 | 32 ms | 160 ms
| st1 1 TiB sustained baseline | 40 | 200 ms | 1,000 ms
| sc1 1 TiB sustained baseline | 12 | 667 ms | 3,333 ms
|===

Add backend/seek/queue time and nonoverlapped application work. Small gp2 volumes have
different capacity-linked limits and credits; the 1 TiB row cannot describe them. SSD
block I/O accounting may split large application reads; count actual I/Os for the IOPS
constraint. Eight concurrent requests share the volume bandwidth, not eight copies of it.
Extra gp3 throughput has a price and provisioning constraints.

Sources checked 2026-10-25:
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/general-purpose.html[SSD limits],
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/hdd-vols.html[HDD baselines],
https://docs.aws.amazon.com/ebs/latest/userguide/ebs-io-characteristics.html[I/O accounting].

Optimistic bound uses provisioned bandwidth with locality; base includes measured
application work and expected utilization; pessimistic uses exhausted credits, competing
traffic and rewarming. No numeric p95/p99 is inferable for unmeasured EBS from this run.
Use real trace local bytes/query; D8's remote 54.7 MB/query is not a measured L2 demand.

This supports testing the uncovered read paths before promising zero-request residency.

== Decision and remaining gate

Keep the current cap until a controlled production-query sweep proves otherwise. Add cap
and gap CLI/reporting to the existing harness, verify actual effective sizes, hold geometry
and query identities fixed, and compare 4/16/40 first, then refine 8/32/64. Record useful
bytes, request mix, waves, recall, CPU, RSS, p95 and sustained QPS. No global 40 MiB flip.
Use gp3 as the first EBS candidate; st1/sc1 require demonstrated sequential locality and
sustained sizing, and gp2 is principally an existing-deployment comparison.
